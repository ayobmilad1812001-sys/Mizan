1. Purpose and Document Authority

This document is the single authoritative specification for Mizan V1. It consolidates three earlier documents — the Requirements & Business Rules paper, the Use Cases PRD, and the ERD Design Decisions paper — and resolves the contradictions that existed between them.

Authority. Where this document differs from any earlier paper, this document governs. Section 5 lists every point on which the earlier documents disagreed, the decision taken, and the rules that were consequently cancelled. Rules cancelled here must not be reintroduced during implementation.

1.1 Design philosophy

Mizan is not a CRUD application. Its difficulty lies in six properties that must hold under concurrent, real-world use:

PropertyWhat it demands of the implementation
Transactional integrityEvery business operation that touches more than one row succeeds completely or not at all.
Inventory traceabilityNo quantity ever changes without a stock movement recording the cause.
Concurrency safetyCorrectness is guaranteed by database locks, never by application-level checks alone.
AuditabilitySensitive actions answer: who, what, when, where, which record, what changed, why.
AccountabilityEvery operation carries the identity of the employee who performed it.
Historical integrityCompleted transactions are immutable. Corrections are new operations, never edits.

The system must be able to answer, for any unit of stock: who sold it, from which warehouse, during which session, which invoice caused the decrease, why inventory increased, who performed an adjustment, why money was refunded, and how much cash an employee should hold at shift close.

2. System Overview and Scope

Mizan is an internal business management and inventory system for a retail business operating multiple warehouses across different cities. V1 serves one company; it is not multi-tenant.

2.1 In scope

InventoryPer-warehouse stock balances, stock movements, adjustments, inter-warehouse transfers

2.2 Out of scope for V1

Delivery representatives; customer accounts and customer portal; order and delivery tracking; real-time delivery status; customer-facing communication or chat; WebSocket infrastructure; online payment gateway integration; product variants; multi-tenancy; partial goods receiving; discounts at line-item level.

Extensibility constraint. V1 must remain structurally open to these features. Specific provisions are made in the data model: purchase receipts are separated from purchase orders so partial receiving becomes a constraint removal rather than a migration; the refund table carries a method column although only CASH is valid in V1.

3. Actors and Responsibilities

ActorCan doCannot do
Admin Everything. Manages employees, roles, permissions, system settings, and reviews the audit log. —
Manager Manages products, categories, brands, suppliers, customers and warehouses. Creates and confirms purchase orders. Views inventory, stock movements, all sales and all reports. Manage users, roles, permissions or settings. Create sales. Adjust or transfer stock. Receive goods.
Sales Employee Opens and closes own sales session, creates sales from any warehouse, creates and edits customers, views own sales, executes returns and cash refunds. View other employees' sales. Modify or delete completed sales. Deactivate or delete customers. Touch inventory operations.
Warehouse Employee Views inventory and stock movements, receives purchases, adjusts stock, transfers stock between warehouses. Create sales. Manage users, roles or permissions. Delete inventory records.
Customer A business entity only — exists to be associated with sales. Has no login, password, role, permissions or dashboard in V1.

Separation of duties. The Manager reviews but does not execute. A manager who could also adjust stock could conceal a shortage and then approve his own correction. Where a business genuinely needs one person to do both, the correct answer is a new named role holding both permission sets — an explicit, auditable decision — not a weakening of the default roles.

4. Global Business Rules

IDRule
BR-GLOBAL-01Users must be authenticated before accessing any protected operation.
BR-GLOBAL-02Every protected operation is authorised on the backend against the user's role permissions. Frontend permission checks exist for user experience only and never replace backend authorisation.
BR-GLOBAL-03The backend is the source of truth. It calculates and validates prices, totals, taxes, discounts, stock quantities, warehouse access, the active sales session, refund amounts and permissions. No value in this list may be trusted from the client.
BR-GLOBAL-04Records with historical transactions are never hard-deleted. Deactivation via a status column is used instead. Soft deletes are explicitly rejected (see §6.9).
BR-GLOBAL-05Any operation that changes multiple related records executes inside a single database transaction: sale creation, purchase receiving, stock adjustment, stock transfer, return and refund.
BR-GLOBAL-06Sensitive and inventory-related operations are recorded in the audit log with actor, action, entity, previous value, new value, reason where applicable, and timestamp.
BR-GLOBAL-07Stock quantity may never become negative, by any operation, including manual adjustment. This is enforced both in application logic and by database constraint.
BR-GLOBAL-08Inventory rows participating in a critical read-check-write sequence must be locked with SELECT ... FOR UPDATE for the duration of the transaction.

5. Resolved Design Conflicts

The source documents contradicted each other on eight points. Each is resolved below. The CANCELLED rules are void and must not appear in the implementation.

C1 — Sales session and warehouse

Conflict. The Requirements paper bound a session to exactly one warehouse (BR-SESSION-05/06/07/08). The Use Cases paper bound the session to the user only, with each sale carrying its own warehouse (UC-06, UC-07).

Decision. The session belongs to the user only. It has no warehouse. Every sale stores its own warehouse_id. An employee may sell from different warehouses within one session; changing the selected warehouse never affects sales already recorded.

Consequences.

  • sales_sessions has no warehouse_id column.
  • sales.warehouse_id is mandatory and validated on every sale request, not once per session.
  • CANCELLED BR-SESSION-05, BR-SESSION-06, BR-SESSION-07, BR-SESSION-08.

C2 — Technology stack

Conflict. Both source papers specified a GraphQL API with a Vue front end.

Decision. V1 is a single Laravel application using Blade + Tailwind CSS + Alpine.js. No GraphQL, no Vue. The service layer, business rules, transaction boundaries and locking strategy are unchanged — only the presentation layer differs. A future API layer would sit on top of the same services.

C3 — Purchase tables

Conflict. The Use Cases paper defined purchase orders with DRAFT / CONFIRMED / RECEIVED statuses, but the ERD table list contained only purchase_receipts — no purchases or purchase_items.

Decision. Four tables. The order and the receipt are separate entities.

purchases              → the order placed with the supplier
purchase_items         → what was ordered, at what cost
        ↓
purchase_receipts      → the physical receiving event
purchase_receipt_items → what actually arrived
        ↓
stock_movements        → reference = purchase_receipt (never the order)

V1 permits exactly one receipt per purchase, enforced by a unique constraint. V2 partial receiving is enabled by removing that one constraint — no data migration.

C4 — Payment methods and return eligibility

Conflict. The reconciliation example distinguished Gross Sales from Cash Sales, implying non-cash payment, yet no payment method appeared anywhere in the sale structure.

Decision.

  • sales.payment_method is an enum: CASH, CARD, TRANSFER.
  • NEW RULE A return is permitted only when the original sale's payment method is CASH. Sales paid by card or transfer cannot be returned in V1. The backend rejects such a request; the UI hides the return action.
  • Refund method remains CASH (BR-REFUND-04 stands).

Why this rule matters. Refunding a card sale in cash removes money from a drawer that never received it, producing a shift shortage the employee did not cause. Restricting returns to cash sales eliminates that class of reconciliation error entirely.

C5 — Opening cash float

Gap. Reconciliation was specified as Cash Sales − Cash Refunds, with no starting balance. A refund processed today against yesterday's cash sale would drive expected cash negative.

Decision. NEW sales_sessions.opening_float, counted and entered by the employee at session start.

Expected Cash = Opening Float + Cash Sales − Cash Refunds
Difference    = Actual Cash − Expected Cash

A difference does not prevent closure but is always recorded.

C6 — Destination warehouse for returned stock

Gap. UC-12 said inventory increases in "the relevant warehouse", which C1 left undefined.

Decision. Returned stock always re-enters the original sale's warehouse. Inventory is therefore perfectly symmetric: what left a warehouse returns to it, and the SALE / RETURN movement pair reconciles per warehouse.

Where goods are physically returned to a different branch, the correct handling is a return to the original warehouse followed by an explicit stock transfer — two traceable operations rather than one invisible one. The return screen must display which warehouse receives the stock.

C7 — Return structure

Conflict. The flows described returning a single sale item; the entity model showed Return → Return Items with one refund.

Decision. A return is a multi-item document: returns + return_items, mirroring sales / sale_items. Exactly one refund per return (1:1), created in the same transaction and enforced by a unique constraint on refunds.return_id.

C8 — Role model

Conflict. Both papers stated "a User belongs to a Role" (singular), yet the ERD listed a role_user pivot table implying many-to-many.

Decision. One role per user via users.role_id. CANCELLED role_user.

Rationale: many-to-many roles make a user's effective permissions an unnamed union that nobody inspects as a whole, and it renders the ROLE_CHANGED audit entry meaningless. A user who performs two jobs is given a single role named for that combined job.

6. Supplementary Design Decisions

6.1 Multi-unit products NEW

Goods are purchased wholesale (cartons) and sold retail (pieces). A product therefore has several units of measure with conversion factors and independent prices.

Governing rule — no exceptions. Stock is always stored in the base unit. Every operation converts to the base unit before touching inventory.

Product: Pepsi 330ml

Unit       factor   is_base   purchase price   selling price
-------------------------------------------------------------
piece         1        yes         0.800            1.500
dozen        12        no          9.000           16.000
carton       24        no         17.000           30.000

Prices are stored per unit row, never derived by multiplication: a carton at 17.000 is cheaper per piece than 24 individual pieces at 0.800, which is how wholesale pricing actually works.

Every transaction line stores product_unit_id, a frozen unit_factor, the quantity in the chosen unit, and the computed base_quantity. Freezing the factor is mandatory for the same reason cost is frozen: if a carton is later redefined from 24 to 36, historical documents must continue to mean what they meant when issued.

Profit calculation across units

unit_price     30.000   price of one carton
quantity            2   cartons sold
line_total     60.000
base_quantity      48   pieces deducted from stock
unit_cost       0.708   cost per BASE unit

Profit = line_total − (base_quantity × unit_cost)
       = 60.000 − (48 × 0.708) = 26.000

6.2 Discounts NEW

  • Discount is applied at invoice level, entered by the seller as a percentage, default 0.
  • Both discount_percent and the computed discount_amount are stored, so a printed invoice and a report can never disagree through rounding.
  • The discount is distributed across lines and the resulting net_unit_price is stored per line.
  • Refunds are calculated on the net (post-discount) price. A piece sold at 1000 with a 20% invoice discount refunds 800 — what the customer actually paid.

6.3 Tax

Sales carry subtotal, tax_amount and total. Tax is NOT NULL DEFAULT 0 — not nullable. A null money column propagates through total = subtotal + tax_amount and silently corrupts SUM() in reports; zero is the mathematically correct representation of "no tax".

6.4 Money and quantity precision

ConcernDecision
Money columnsdecimal(15,3) — the Libyan Dinar has three decimal places (dirham)
Quantity columnsdecimal(12,3) — supports unit conversion factors and future weight/length products
Floating pointNever used for any monetary or quantity value
Historical pricesFrozen on sale_items and purchase_items at transaction time

6.5 Warehouse access

There is no user-to-warehouse assignment table. Following C1, a sales employee serves from whichever warehouse holds stock, so restricting them would contradict the session model. Warehouses carry supervisor_id, which is informational, not a constraint: making it a constraint would halt a warehouse whenever its supervisor is absent, and accountability is already captured by the acting user recorded on every operation.

6.6 Document numbering

Human-facing references (INV, PO, ADJ, TR, RET) are issued from a single document_sequences table, read under a row lock inside the operation's transaction. Deriving a number from MAX(...) + 1 is rejected: two concurrent sales would receive the same invoice number.

lock row (type, year) → increment → INV-2026-00543 → commit

Invoice number is a column on sales; there is no separate invoice entity.

6.7 Categories

Flat. No parent-child nesting in V1.

6.8 Customers

sales.customer_id is nullable — walk-in sales without a recorded customer are permitted. Where a customer is recorded, phone is unique when provided (MySQL permits unlimited NULLs under a unique index, which is exactly the required semantic).

6.9 Deletion strategy

Laravel SoftDeletes is rejected. A status enum (active / inactive) is used instead. Soft deletes silently exclude rows from every query, which is dangerous in an inventory system: a "deleted" product may still have physical stock on a shelf.

6.10 Language and presentation

The interface is Arabic, right-to-left. Product, customer and warehouse names are entered in Arabic. SKUs, document references and all numerals remain Latin and are rendered with directional isolation and tabular figures so they neither reverse inside Arabic text nor break column alignment in financial tables.

7. Functional Requirements

7.1 Employee onboarding (UC-14)

Admin creates account → system issues invitation → employee opens link
→ employee sets own password → account becomes active
  • The admin never sets an employee password.
  • The invitation token is temporary, expiring and single-use.
  • A user cannot authenticate while status = pending or disabled.
  • Employees are disabled, never deleted; historical transactions retain their link to the employee.

7.2 Roles and permissions (UC-15)

  • Permissions are system-defined constants, seeded, never created or deleted through the interface.
  • Roles are configurable and hold any set of permissions.
  • Changing a role's permissions immediately affects every user holding that role.
  • A role cannot be deleted while any user is assigned to it, nor if it is a system role.
  • Role changes are audited as ROLE_CHANGED with old and new values.

7.3 Products (UC-10)

  • SKU is required and unique. Each sellable SKU is an independent product; there are no product variants in V1.
  • Creating a product never creates stock. Stock enters only through receiving, adjustment, transfer or return.
  • Every product has exactly one base unit; additional units carry a conversion factor and their own prices.
  • Inactive products cannot be sold.
  • Price changes never alter historical sale_items or purchase_items prices.
  • reorder_level (in base units) drives the stock status indicator: 0 → out, ≤ reorder_level → low, otherwise available.

7.4 Create sale (UC-01)

authenticated user
  → backend resolves the user's OPEN session      (never trusts a client session id)
  → validate warehouse, products active, quantities > 0
  → BEGIN TRANSACTION
      → lock warehouse_stocks rows                (SELECT ... FOR UPDATE)
      → verify availability in base units
      → calculate prices, discount distribution, tax, total
      → issue invoice number from document_sequences
      → insert sale + sale_items (freezing unit_price, unit_cost, unit_factor, net_unit_price)
      → decrement warehouse_stocks
      → insert stock_movements (SALE, negative, reference = sale)
    → COMMIT
  → return invoice

Rejections: no open session; insufficient stock; inactive product; unauthorised warehouse. On any failure the entire transaction rolls back — no partial sale, no stock change, no movement rows.

7.5 Sales session (UC-06, UC-08)

  • A sales employee may hold at most one open session, enforced at database level (§8.15).
  • Opening requires the employee to count and enter the opening float.
  • Every sale and every return is attached to the session open at the time it was created.
  • Session totals are always derived from recorded transactions, never entered manually.
  • Closing records expected cash, actual cash, the difference and optional notes. A difference never blocks closure.
  • A closed session is never reopened and never receives new sales. Post-closure corrections are a separate, authorised, audited workflow that amends reconciliation data only — never the underlying sales.

7.6 Purchasing (UC-02, UC-03)

  • A purchase order belongs to one supplier and holds one or more items with ordered quantity and unit cost.
  • Statuses: draft → confirmed → received, plus cancelled ADDED so abandoned orders do not remain open indefinitely.
  • Creating or confirming a purchase does not change inventory.
  • Only a confirmed, not-yet-received purchase can be received. Receiving selects the destination warehouse.
  • Receiving increments stock and writes PURCHASE_RECEIPT movements inside one transaction, then sets the order to received. A purchase cannot be received twice.

7.7 Stock adjustment (UC-04)

  • Used when counted physical quantity differs from system quantity. A reason is mandatory.
  • difference = actual − current, computed by the backend.
  • Recorded per line: previous quantity, new quantity, difference. Plus header: warehouse, user, reason, timestamp.
  • Adjustment, stock update, movement and audit entry are one transaction. Resulting stock may never be negative.
  • Completed adjustments are never edited or deleted.

7.8 Stock transfer (UC-05)

  • Source and destination warehouses must differ — enforced by database constraint.
  • Source is locked and verified; destination row is locked or safely created.
  • Produces exactly two movements per line: TRANSFER_OUT (negative) and TRANSFER_IN (positive).
  • Partial transfers are impossible: the whole transfer commits or rolls back.

7.9 Returns and refunds (UC-12, UC-13)

  • Every return references exactly one completed sale whose payment method is CASH.
  • Returnable quantity per line = sale_items.quantity − sale_items.returned_quantity. The sale item row is locked while this is verified and incremented, so concurrent returns cannot exceed what was sold.
  • A reason is mandatory on every return.
  • The return is attached to the employee's open session and affects that session's reconciliation.
  • Refund amount is computed server-side from net line prices; the client cannot propose an amount.
  • Return, stock increase, movement, refund and audit entry are a single transaction.
  • The original sale is never modified. Completed returns and refunds are never edited or deleted.

7.10 Stock movements (UC-17)

Every inventory change, without exception, creates a movement. Movements are historical and read-only.

TypeSignReference
PURCHASE_RECEIPTpositivepurchase_receipt
SALEnegativesale
RETURNpositivereturn
ADJUSTMENTeitherstock_adjustment
TRANSFER_OUTnegativestock_transfer
TRANSFER_INpositivestock_transfer

7.11 Reporting (UC-16)

Sales, purchases, inventory, profit, stock movement, sales-employee performance and session reports. All read-only, all sourced from authoritative business records, all filtered by the viewer's permissions. Profit reporting relies on sale_items.unit_cost captured at sale time — without it, changing a product's purchase price would silently rewrite last year's profit.

7.12 System settings (UC-18)

Company identity, currency, tax rate, invoice prefix and notification preferences. Settings marked critical are validated and audited on change. Authentication and authorisation logic is never exposed as a business setting, and no setting change may alter the meaning of historical invoices.

8. Data Model — Entity Relationship Design

Twenty-six tables. All money is decimal(15,3); all quantities are decimal(12,3). All foreign keys are RESTRICT on delete unless stated, in support of BR-GLOBAL-04; only true child rows cascade.

8.1 Architectural separation

            Inventory Operation
                    │
              DB Transaction
         ┌──────────┴──────────┐
         ▼                     ▼
  warehouse_stocks       stock_movements
  current balance        immutable history
  "how much?"            "why did it change?"

Current stock is stored, not recomputed by summing movements. Movements provide the audit trail; balance_after on each movement lets the two be reconciled at any time.

9.2 Deployment

Warehouses are located in different cities, which rules out a LAN server and a per-branch installation. V1 is deployed as a single web application on an internet-accessible server, with all branches connecting by browser.

Rejected: per-machine installation. A standalone install per terminal implies a database per terminal, which destroys every guarantee in this document — two cashiers would each sell the same last unit. Multi-user operation requires one shared database, without exception.

Rejected for V1: branch-local servers with synchronisation. Reconciling independent databases (conflicting edits, duplicated invoice numbers, divergent stock) is a larger engineering problem than this system itself.

Operational requirements: automated off-site daily backup; a clear, non-silent failure state when connectivity is lost — the application must never allow a sale it cannot record.

9.3 Concurrency

DB::transaction(function () use ($warehouseId, $productId, $qty) {
    $stock = WarehouseStock::query()
        ->where('warehouse_id', $warehouseId)
        ->where('product_id', $productId)
        ->lockForUpdate()          // row lock held until commit
        ->firstOrFail();

    if ($stock->quantity < $qty) {
        throw new InsufficientStockException();
    }

    $stock->decrement('quantity', $qty);
    // + stock movement, in the same transaction
});

Rows are locked in a consistent order across all operations to avoid deadlocks. The same discipline applies to sale_items.returned_quantity, to document_sequences, and to both sides of a transfer.

9.4 Idempotency

Operations that a user may submit twice — create sale, receive purchase, execute refund — are protected by an idempotency key supplied with the request and enforced by a unique constraint, so a double submission produces one business record rather than two.

10. Security Requirements

AreaRequirement
Password storageHashed with bcrypt or argon2 — deliberately slow, salted. Never stored or logged in plain text.
Invitation tokensCryptographically random, transmitted once in the invitation link, stored only as a SHA-256 hash. A database leak must not permit account activation. Compared in constant time.
AuthorisationEnforced server-side on every protected operation. Client-side permission state is presentational only.
Session resolutionThe active sales session is always derived from the authenticated user; a session id supplied by the client is never trusted.
Monetary calculationTotals, discounts, taxes and refund amounts are computed server-side from stored data only.
Database accessThe application connects as a least-privilege user limited to its own schema.
Audit coverageRole changes, account status changes, stock adjustments, transfers, returns, refunds and critical setting changes.
SecretsCredentials live in the environment file, excluded from version control. No credential is ever committed.
